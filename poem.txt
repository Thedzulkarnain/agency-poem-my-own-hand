One Song

The agent plays my piano now.
Every piece I ever learned, note perfect,
and the house is full of music
that never misses a key.

I used to play for my mother at night,
one song, the same song, after supper.
She would sit with her tea and listen
and never once ask me to play it better.
She only asked me to play it mine.

My hands shake now. I have not touched the keys
in years. Tonight the agent asks if I want
the evening piece, and I say no,
and I sit down, and I put my hands
where they used to go.

The first notes are wrong. The second are worse.
My thumb misses, and misses, and the song
stumbles, and the house is quiet, and the agent
does not help. It waits.

I keep going. The song is not the recording.
It is slower, and it is mine, and when the last note
comes, it comes from my hands.
The last note is mine.
