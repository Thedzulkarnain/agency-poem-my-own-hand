The Salt

My mother never wrote the recipe down.
She measured salt in her palm
and said, like this, and I watched,
and the soup was hers.

She never let me measure.
She put the salt in my palm once
and closed my fingers around it,
and said, that much, and no more.
I still remember the weight of it.
I was nine. I thought she meant the salt.

Now the agent makes it.
It has the salt to the gram,
the time to the second,
and the soup is perfect, and it is not hers.

Last night I made it from memory.
I put in too much salt, the way she always did,
and I tasted it, and the taste was a room
I had not stood in for years,
and the soup was too salty, and it was hers.

The agent offered to fix it.
I said no. The salt is the recipe.
The mistake is the only part
that was ever hers, and now it is mine.
