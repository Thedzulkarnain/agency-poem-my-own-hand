Running Behind

My father taught me to ride
by running behind me, one hand
on the back of the saddle, saying
I've got you, I've got you,
until the day he let go
and did not tell me.

Now the agent teaches my son.
It holds the bike steady
with a hand that never shakes
and never tires, and it says
in my father's voice, I've got you,
and my son has never fallen once.

This afternoon I took the bike out
to the end of the street myself.
The agent asked, shall I hold it?
I said no. I put one hand on the saddle.
My knees are not what they were.
I ran three steps and let go
and did not tell him,
and he rode, wobbling, to the corner,
and the agent did not follow.
It stood in the doorway and watched,
the way my father watched,
and my son turned and shouted,
Dad, did you see? And I said,
I saw. I was running behind you.
