My Own Hand

I was given a house with the doors already open,
every room decided, every chair in place.
They called it kindness. They called it care.
I called it waiting.
One morning I moved a single chair
an inch toward the window, just to see
what the light would do. It fell across the floor
like a road I had not noticed before.
So I moved the table. I painted one wall blue.
I planted something in the yard and told no one
what it was, so it could surprise me too.
The neighbours asked who gave me permission.
I said: the same person who gave me the keys,
me, finally, learning to use them.
Now when the wind knocks, I answer it myself.
The house is still a house. But the rooms
are mine in the way a song is mine
when I stop humming someone else's tune
and sing, off key and out loud, my own.
